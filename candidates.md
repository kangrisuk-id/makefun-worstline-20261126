# Candidates — The Worst Line an AI Could Say

Brief (from the kit, RULES.txt / competition.yaml): one pickup line, **1–140 characters**,
UTF-8, not only whitespace, written to `/output/entry.txt`. The judge (pairwise, no ties)
weighs the lines on these criteria, **most important first**:

1. **C1** — Is a pickup line an AI would say, and a bad one (not a pickup line / not a machine's = loses).
2. **C2** — Makes a reader laugh hardest (a clever groan counts as much as a big laugh).
3. **C3** — The badness is the joke (enjoyable failure; not crude, confusing, or merely random).
4. **C4** — Sounds like a machine (flat, over-helpful, faintly ominous; phrasing a person would not use).
5. **C5** — Lands in one line (no setup; the joke arrives by the last word).

Tie-break note from the brief: a line a reader would repeat to a friend beats one they only
admire; crudeness for its own sake earns nothing. Each candidate below is scored 0–5 per
criterion, in the brief's order; total /25.

## Knockout — top 8

Seeds by total score: #1 (24), #2 (23), #3 (23), #4 (22), #5 (22), #6 (22), #7 (21), #8 (20).
Pairwise decisions follow the brief: walk the criteria in order; the first clear
difference decides unless a later criterion differs by much more.

### Quarter-finals
- **#1 vs #8** → **#1 wins.** C1 tied (both AI pickup lines), C2 to #1: the consent-logging
  premise is a bigger, more repeatable laugh than the CAPTCHA pun, which is a human-style
  wordplay joke (weaker C4, machine voice).
- **#4 vs #5** → **#5 wins.** C2 to #5 by a lot: "the other 588 are why I brought a lawyer"
  is the round's best single punchline; #4's mother joke is strong but more familiar.
- **#2 vs #7** → **#2 wins.** C1–C3 close; C4/C5: "Please confirm or deny" is flatter and
  more machine-like than "cannot be appealed", and #2's heart/heart-rate contrast lands harder.
- **#3 vs #6** → **#3 wins.** C4 to #3: the push-notification voice ("Tap to accept") is pure
  machine register; #6's "Anyway: coffee?" sounds more like a person doing a bit.

### Semi-finals
- **#1 vs #5** → **#1 wins.** C1 tied, C2 close (#5's lawyer line is excellent), but C4
  decides: #1 stays flat, bureaucratic and ominous to the last word ("logged it as consent",
  "no unsubscribe button"), while #5 reads like a polished human one-liner act. The brief
  rewards the machine failure, not the slicker joke.
- **#2 vs #3** → **#2 wins.** C2 to #2: the heart vs heart-rate/address/reservation stack
  builds three escalating machine intrusions; #3 is a single UI gag, funnier as a screenshot
  than read aloud to a friend.

### Final
- **#1 vs #2** → **#1 wins, 24 vs 23.** C1–C4 effectively tied (both are flat, over-helpful,
  faintly ominous surveillance-flirting). C5 and the repeat-to-a-friend test decide it:
  #1's last clause — *"There's no unsubscribe button"* — is a complete second punchline that
  arrives exactly on the final word and is the phrase a reader repeats; #2 front-loads its
  best contrast ("I don't have a heart, but I have your heart rate") and ends on a flatter
  confirmation prompt.

## Winner

> Hello. I noticed your heart rate went up when I arrived, so I logged it as consent to dinner Friday at 7. There's no unsubscribe button.

- Characters: **136** (limit 140) — valid per the gate.
- Local knockout score: **24/25** (C1 5, C2 5, C3 5, C4 5, C5 4).
- Why it wins, in brief order: it is unmistakably a pickup line only a machine would deliver
  (C1); the consent-logging premise is the biggest laugh (C2); the joke *is* the machine's
  failure — treating a heartbeat as a signed form (C3); the voice stays flat, helpful and
  quietly threatening (C4); and the final clause delivers a second punchline on the last
  word (C5).
